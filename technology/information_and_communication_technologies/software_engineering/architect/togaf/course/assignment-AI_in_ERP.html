Significant constructs under the subject of Artificial Intelligence (AI) leveraged in the industry today are Machine Learning (ML) and Large Language Models (LLMs). In the scope of Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems, the following are some of the areas in which traditional ML is applied:

  • Inventory Optimization

  • Demand Forecasting

  • Fraud Detection and Prevention

  • Invoice Extraction

  • Recommendation Systems

Some areas where LLMs (especially Reasoning Models) find applications are:

  • Invoice Classification

  • Chat Agents - for querying the ERP in Natural Language

  • Report Generation

  • Policy/Contract Interpretation

This has resulted in AI augmenting ERP systems as a mechanism to observe system behaviour and obtain decision points in a manner that is easier for business stakeholders to work with. Using these as a reference, and provided that cloud enablement for the ERP modules has been completed, the potential use cases for AI for each of the modules in question can hypothetically be as listed below.

Finance & Accounting

Forecasting (using RNNs)

  • Revenue

  • Expense

  • Accounts Payable

  • Accounts Receivable

  • Working Capital

Risk Analysis and Prevention (using Random Forests, LSTMs, GNNs, and GenAI)

  • Duplicate Transaction Prevention

  • Identification of Anomalous Transaction Patterns


Improved resilience through monitoring and prediction capabilities.

Inventory Management

There are particularly strong use cases, as traditional algorithms (using Weighted Moving Average and smoothing functions, along with Economic Order Quantity and Economic Production Quantity) can be replaced or augmented with a mix of Machine Learning/Deep Learning mechanisms and Generative AI/Reasoning Models. The three largest challenges in inventory management and mechanisms (hypothetical) that can be used are:

  • Seasonality & Trends: Temporal Fusion Transformers (TFTs, specialized Deep Learning algorithms) for predicting future demand, inventory turnover, and stockout probabilities

  • Product Bundling: Agents for persona-based Market Basket Analysis with support of Graph Databases

  • Assortment Planning: Traditional Choice Modelling augmented with Reasoning Models for shelf-space optimization while accounting for business-rule compliance


Improved reliability in managing inventory.

Procurement

The following are some areas where AI can be utilized:

  • Time to Market: Automated RFP/RFQ drafting

  • Reducing Stockouts: Predictive scoring mechanisms for vendors based on historical data points (product quality, delivery reliability, and cost competitiveness)

  • Dead Stock Reduction: RNNs and TFTs for improved inventory ranging and demand prediction, thereby improving RFP/RFQ recommendations


Reduced risk and improved availability when managing procurement.

Manufacturing

There are already some strong use cases for using AI in this field, some of which, along with some hypothetical use cases, are:

  • Predictive Maintenance: Using RNNs to accurately predict maintenance requirements and maintenance schedules for machines and their components

  • Machine, Material and Labour Availability: Predicting unplanned downtime and production constraints using LSTMs and TFTs

  • Labour Augmentation: Quality inspection automation (YOLO), and Root Cause Analysis (RAG augmenting pattern recognition)


More reliable manufacturing processes with tighter feedback loops.

Supply Chain Management

Supply Chain Management provides one of the strongest opportunities for applying AI across ERP modules, primarily because supply-chain decisions depend on information originating from Finance, Inventory, Procurement and Manufacturing. Some potential use cases are:

  • Supply Chain Risk Prediction: Using ML models to identify potential disruptions caused by supplier delays, material shortages, capacity constraints, demand fluctuations and logistics disruptions.

  • End-to-End Demand and Supply Planning: Combining demand forecasts, inventory positions, supplier lead times, manufacturing capacity and customer orders to identify potential supply-demand imbalances before they occur.

  • What-If Scenario Analysis: Using simulation and reasoning models to evaluate the downstream impact of events such as supplier delays, demand spikes, production outages or transportation disruptions.

  • Dynamic Logistics Optimization: Optimizing transportation routes, shipment consolidation, delivery schedules and carrier selection based on cost, capacity, lead time and service-level requirements.

  • Supply Chain Agents: Using AI agents to continuously monitor supply-chain events, identify exceptions, correlate their impact across ERP modules, and recommend appropriate corrective actions.

  • Root Cause and Impact Analysis: Using RAG and reasoning models to correlate transactional, operational and historical data in order to explain the root cause and potential business impact of supply-chain exceptions.


Improved supply-chain resilience through predictive risk identification, scenario analysis, and coordinated decision-making across ERP modules.

The Target Architecture

Given the use cases, I would recommend the following stages, as a roadmap to the target architecture:

  1. Cloud ERP(Current): ERP systems fully deployed to cloud, with centralized data availability & Omni-Channel enablement
  2. AI augmented ERP: Predictive & Prescreptive AI leveraging the data
  3. Agentic ERP(Target): Autonomous workflows orchestrated via a team of AI agents
The architecture vision I would put it as is:
An AI-augmented, increasingly agentic cloud ERP in which Finance, Inventory, Procurement, Manufacturing and Supply Chain share a common enterprise intelligence layer that can understand business context, predict future states, recommend actions and execute controlled workflows while the ERP remains the authoritative transactional system.
